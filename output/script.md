# What if the Sun vanished?

What if the Sun vanished? For about eight minutes and twenty seconds, you'd notice nothing. Sunlight already in space would still be racing toward Earth. Then daylight would end. In this impossible thought experiment, changes in gravity also travel at light speed. Earth would leave its solar orbit, moving roughly along a tangent. But we wouldn't instantly freeze. The oceans and atmosphere store heat. Without sunlight, photosynthesis would stop, and the surface would keep cooling. Now the twist. Life could survive. Around seafloor vents, some microbes use chemical energy instead of sunlight. Earth's internal heat wouldn't disappear with the Sun. Our world could lose its star, without immediately losing every living thing.

## Editorial guardrails
The disappearance violates conservation of mass-energy: this is explicitly an impossible thought experiment, not a prediction or a solar explosion. The frozen landscape is a long-term illustrative outcome with no cooling deadline asserted. Survival means some microbial life could persist, not that all vent animals or humans would survive indefinitely.
